Section A

Core manufacturing value streams

Use these process lenses to identify handoffs, decision points, system touchpoints, and KPIs that matter most for a client conversation.

Design to retire

Connect ideation, design governance, engineering release, service history, and end-of-life decisions so product data stays usable throughout the lifecycle.

Focus: BOM governance, design traceability, product updates, field feedback, retirement planning.

Acquire to dispose

Cover supplier onboarding, purchasing, inbound logistics, inventory usage, asset handling, and controlled disposal of excess or obsolete materials.

Focus: sourcing strategy, supplier risk, inbound quality, procurement compliance, scrap reduction.

Forecast to plan

Translate demand signals into consensus plans that balance service targets, capacity constraints, supply risk, and scenario-based decision making.

Focus: demand sensing, S&OP/S&OE, planning accuracy, exception management.

Plan to produce

Move from master planning into production orders, line scheduling, labor and machine sequencing, material staging, and execution readiness.

Focus: finite capacity, campaign planning, sequence optimization, labor utilization.

Order to cash

Follow customer commitments from quote and order capture through promising, fulfillment, invoicing, and post-delivery financial reconciliation.

Focus: ATP/CTP, margin protection, order changes, invoice accuracy, cash collection.

Inventory to deliver

Optimize storage, replenishment, warehouse flow, transport planning, and outbound execution while maintaining traceability and service performance.

Focus: inventory health, warehouse productivity, OTIF, shipment visibility.

Service to deliver

Coordinate maintenance, spare parts, field service, and warranty obligations to protect uptime and create feedback loops for product improvement.

Focus: installed base visibility, service parts planning, work order execution, service margins.

How to use the process page

Start with the flow that is currently under the most pressure, then trace upstream and downstream dependencies. This helps identify whether the primary bottleneck is data quality, planning, execution, or organizational alignment.

Typical workshop prompts

  • Where do planners or operators rely on spreadsheets outside the system landscape?
  • Which handoffs create avoidable delay, rework, or expediting?
  • What data needs to stay synchronized across engineering, supply chain, and production?
